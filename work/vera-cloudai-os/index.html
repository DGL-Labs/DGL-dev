What it is

Vera sits between the AI a team already uses and the systems the business runs on. It decides what AI is allowed to change, and it keeps the evidence of what actually happened.

It is not another model. Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and whatever follows supply the intelligence. Vera is the layer underneath, where a decision turns into a change in a real system. It runs today against Google Workspace, and other business systems are in development.

Why it exists

AI has started doing the work rather than describing it - updating records, changing documents, sending messages, running processes that used to need a person. That is a different problem from getting a good answer. Once AI can change things, somebody has to decide how much authority it gets, and somebody has to be able to check the work.

Most teams answer that by keeping AI on a short leash and checking everything by hand. It works until the volume grows, and then it quietly stops working.

The three jobs, in the order they matter

Know. Company information changes constantly, and last year's document sits in Drive looking exactly as official as this year's. Vera marks what the company treats as current and what is history, and every answer carries where it came from.

Control. Every automated change is declared in advance - which system, which part of it, and which process owns it. Anything not on the list does not run, and something unrecognised stops the job rather than defaulting to allowed.

Verify. A success message is not proof. Vera confirms it is pointed at the right thing before it writes, compares what it meant to do against what the system reports afterwards, and records how strong each check was rather than treating every confirmation as equally good.

We learned this by getting it wrong

One of our own automated processes updated a management workbook every fifteen minutes. It ran roughly eleven thousand six hundred times across a hundred and twenty-one days and reported success every time. It had been writing to the wrong file since April.

Separately, an operation set out to update twenty-four cells. The request was accepted, fourteen were saved, and that was reported as a success too.

Nothing crashed and no alert fired, because from the outside everything looked fine, which is precisely what made it dangerous. The rule the product was rebuilt around came out of that: permission to attempt something is not proof that it happened correctly.

Why it is relevant to client work

When a client asks for AI inside their business, the hard part is not the model. It is what the AI is allowed to touch, who authorised it, and how anyone proves afterwards that the work was actually done.

We had to answer that for ourselves first, on our own operations, after our own failure. That is the same argument as the rest of this portfolio: we do not recommend an approach we have not had to operate.

What is built and what is being built

Vera runs Digital Galactica Labs' own operations - not a pilot and not a demo environment, but the real thing, with real consequences when it is wrong.

The commercial platform is still being built, and veracloudai.com lists what is not available yet rather than implying otherwise: multi-company separation, enforced customer sign-in, a broad catalogue of connected systems, and the general boundary through which any AI tool's actions would pass. We would rather say which is which than let a case study blur it.

Capabilities demonstrated

What this project proves we can do

  • Declared-in-advance authority: an unrecognised target stops the job rather than defaulting to allowed
  • Independent resource identification before a write, by identifier and by name
  • Verification that records its own basis, so a self-report is not stored as an independent check
  • Idempotency keys and version preconditions, so a retry cannot execute twice
  • An append-only record of what was attempted, against what, by which process, and how it was verified
  • Model-agnostic by design: changing which AI does the thinking does not change the controls or the record
