Custom Software Development

When existing software does not fit the way your business works, we build software that does.

All services

Most companies do not set out to build custom software. They arrive at it after off the shelf tools have been stretched past what they were designed for, and the workarounds have quietly become the process.

We design and develop custom business applications, internal tools, customer portals, data systems, dashboards, backend services, and software that connects existing systems.

The goal is not more software. The goal is solving the problem.

Problems this solves

You may recognise some of these.

  • A process runs on spreadsheets that only one person fully understands
  • Your team copies the same information between two systems every day
  • The tool you bought does 70 percent of the job and blocks the other 30
  • Reporting takes days because the data lives in four places
  • Growth is limited by how many people you can put on manual work

Process

How an engagement runs

  1. Understand the problem

    What is going wrong, who it affects, and what it costs today. Not a feature list.

  2. Scope the first useful slice

    The smallest thing that changes the situation, rather than a full system on paper.

  3. Build it and put it in front of people

    Working software in production surfaces the constraints nobody mentioned.

  4. Make it operable

    Monitoring, deploys, environments and documentation are part of the build.

  5. Support what happens next

    Software that gets used changes. That is planned for, not discovered.

Questions

Things people ask before starting

We are not sure we need custom software. Is that a problem?
No, and it is a normal place to start. Some clients arrive with a specification, others with a business problem. Both work. If an existing product solves it better and cheaper, we will say so before you pay us to build anything.
Can you work with software we already have?
Yes. A large share of the work is extending, integrating with, or replacing part of an existing system rather than starting from an empty repository.
What happens after the software is delivered?
That is part of the design rather than an afterthought. Deployment, monitoring, environments and documentation are built in so the software can be operated by whoever becomes responsible for it.
